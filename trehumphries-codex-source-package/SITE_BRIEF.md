# TreHumphries.com — Site Brief

## Project objective

Rebuild `trehumphries.com` into the commercial headquarters for a small, premium independent professional practice.

The site has three jobs:

1. Convert service businesses and local brands into fixed-scope web/digital projects.
2. Establish immediate credibility for occasional higher-value controls engineering consulting.
3. Present Tre as a serious independent practitioner with real shipped work — not a generic freelancer, full-service marketing agency, SaaS founder, or résumé site.

The design must support a calm, bounded consulting model. Do not create messaging that implies Tre wants ongoing system ownership, unlimited support, on-call obligations, or an agency-scale client roster.

---

## Core positioning

Public brand: **Tre Humphries**

Tre's useful through-line is not “I do lots of unrelated technology.” It is:

> He understands real businesses and technical systems, turns messy requirements into clear working artifacts, and hands them off cleanly.

That through-line can support two distinct buyer paths without forcing them into the same pitch.

---

# 1. Web & Digital

## Audience

Established service businesses and local brands whose real-world operation is more mature/professional than their current digital presence.

Examples include technical trades, restoration, landscaping, roofing, service contractors, food businesses, beauty/personal services, and other owner-operated businesses with real customers and a credible operating business.

The ideal customer is not necessarily defined by revenue. Useful observable signals include:
- established operations
- real staff/crews/equipment/location or other visible operating substance
- meaningful reputation or customer history
- high-value services or real transaction volume
- reachable owner/decision maker
- digital presence that does not adequately represent the business

New businesses can still buy the offer, but the brand should benchmark visually against established businesses rather than “starter websites.”

## Core offer

**Fixed-Scope Website & Search Foundation**

Typical scope may include:
- strategy/site architecture
- responsive custom design/build
- core service or offering pages
- service-area/business structure where relevant
- contact/quote/intake flow
- CMS where genuinely useful
- foundational on-page search/SEO structure
- analytics/Search Console setup where appropriate
- launch
- client ownership
- documentation/clean handoff

Custom workflows, intake tools, search strategy, or other digital systems may become separate scoped projects where justified.

## Commercial model

- Website engagements typically start around **$3,000**.
- Final pricing is fixed upfront based on scope.
- Do not publish hourly pricing.
- Do not require an ongoing retainer.
- Do not imply Tre will remain the permanent website/IT person.

## Primary message direction

A strong conceptual line is:

> **Your business should look as good online as it does in real life.**

This is a direction, not mandatory final copy. Improve it if a sharper, more specific version emerges from the actual work.

Supporting positioning can convey:

> Websites and digital systems for established service businesses and local brands.

Avoid generic phrases such as “digital transformation,” “elevating brands,” “unlocking growth,” “10x leads,” or “full-service marketing.”

## Conversion

Primary CTA: **Schedule a Project Call**

Secondary CTA: project inquiry/email.

The site should not rely on unexpected phone calls as the default sales experience.

# 2. Controls Engineering

## Audience

OEMs, controls teams, integrators, engineering managers, and technically sophisticated operators that need contained senior engineering support.

## Core offer

**Controls Audit & Design-for-Hire**

Use the attached customer-facing overview as the factual source of truth.

Important principles:
- one system/panel/machine/project per engagement
- audit OR design, not an ambiguous all-purpose engagement
- fixed scope and defined deliverables
- typically 3–10 business days depending on scope
- fixed fee, commonly $3,000–$15,000 depending on complexity
- senior review/design clarity
- clean handoff
- no ongoing on-call/operational support
- no open-ended maintenance ownership
- implementation only if separately scoped

Possible technical themes supported by the offer:
- sequencing/control intent
- IO/signal assumptions
- interlocks/fault handling
- documentation/program-behavior gaps
- handoff risk
- sequence of operations
- state/mode definitions
- edge cases
- commissioning/verification planning

## Secondary bounded offer

**Process & Information Flow Audit**

Use the attached PDF as source of truth.

It should not become a broad “digital transformation” pitch. It is intentionally limited to one workflow and one practical improvement, with clean closeout and optional separately scoped follow-on work.

## Conversion

Primary CTA: **Schedule a Controls Consultation**
Secondary CTA: email Tre.

The controls page must work independently. A serious engineering buyer should not need to interpret bakery/beauty/web work to understand Tre's controls credibility.

# 3. Homepage

The homepage should establish Tre's reputation first and make the two relevant paths obvious.

Do not make it a résumé dump.
Do not make it a giant split-screen “choose Web or Controls” gimmick.
Do not make the web and controls disciplines look like separate companies.

Likely hierarchy:
- strong Tre/name/positioning opening
- concise statement of what he builds/solves
- selected work with HomeEMS receiving major visual weight
- strong entry into Web & Digital
- credible entry into Controls Engineering
- concise personal/technical credibility
- final scheduling/contact CTA

Web may receive somewhat more homepage emphasis because it is the actively marketed lane, but controls should remain visibly legitimate.

Within ~10–15 seconds a web prospect should think:
> “This person builds serious websites for businesses like mine.”

An engineering prospect following a direct controls link should think:
> “This is a real controls engineer offering bounded senior-level help.”

# 4. Work / case studies

The portfolio is sales proof, not an archive.

Do not preserve everything automatically.
Do not show every project at the same size.
Do not rely on technology-stack badges as proof of value.

A useful case-study structure:
- Context / Problem
- Approach
- What I Built
- Outcome / Handoff
- supporting screenshots/artifacts

Never invent metrics. If there is no supported performance metric, describe the tangible deliverable, clearer workflow, new capability, or clean handoff.

Likely content hierarchy:

## Flagship
**HomeEMS**

Use the approved `source/case-studies/homeems.md` as the narrative source of truth.
The finished page should be visual and concise rather than reproducing the entire source article.

## Strong supporting work
- Brazilian Sweet Bites custom order system — workflow/custom-tool proof
- Mayara — polished web/design proof
- Brazilian Sweet Bites — local brand/food proof
- All Seasons — older established service-business proof, used selectively
- 3–5 strongest controls projects from the current site

Codex should audit first before deciding which supporting projects deserve medium case studies versus project cards.

# 5. Visual identity

There is no external aesthetic reference to copy.
Derive the identity from the commercial context.

Overall characteristics:
- premium
- controlled
- clear
- human
- technically literate
- commercially credible
- contemporary
- direct

The web side can be warmer and more visual.
The controls side can be more restrained, diagrammatic, and technical.
They must still share one design system.

Use:
- excellent typography
- strong hierarchy
- generous but purposeful whitespace
- high-quality project imagery
- thoughtful cropping
- clean grids
- restrained color
- subtle motion only where useful
- excellent mobile design

Avoid:
- typical contractor-marketing-agency visuals
- generic SaaS illustrations
- fake analytics dashboards
- loud gradients
- generic stock crews/roofers
- excessive trust badges
- giant service-icon walls
- visually clever but commercially confusing layouts
- sterile minimalist design that feels aimed at designers rather than business owners

Do not create unnecessary logo symbolism. A strong name/wordmark treatment is sufficient.

---

# 6. About / credibility

Tre can appear personally in the site.
Use a strong portrait where it helps trust.

Keep About concise and relevant to buyers.

Useful differentiators include:
- mechanical engineering foundation
- controls/automation experience
- systems thinking across physical and digital systems
- experience with operational businesses
- ability to understand complicated workflows and communicate them simply
- direct client relationship
- clean, bounded delivery and handoff

Do not turn the About page into a full chronological CV.

---

# 7. Geography

Do not position the web practice as North Jersey-only.
Tre may actively market in New Jersey, St. Louis/Missouri, Virginia, and other U.S. markets.

A quiet statement such as “Based in New Jersey, working with businesses across the U.S.” is acceptable if it fits naturally.

Do not build speculative city/market SEO pages yet.

---

# 8. Technical/build principles

The current site is a lightweight React/Cloudflare-style deployment and should remain low-overhead where practical.

Priorities:
- fast
- accessible
- responsive
- easy for Tre/Codex to maintain
- clean source control
- reusable structured project/case-study content
- simple addition of future work
- good metadata/Open Graph
- analytics hooks where appropriate
- no expensive platform migration without a concrete reason
- no CMS unless it solves a real maintenance need
- no unnecessary dependencies
- no overengineering

This is a credibility/sales asset, not a software product.

---

# 9. Copy style

Voice:
- calm
- direct
- concise
- confident
- practical
- knowledgeable
- peer-to-peer

Avoid:
- hype
- “passionate about” filler
- “innovative solutions”
- “digital experiences that elevate brands”
- “transform your business”
- “unlock potential”
- fake urgency
- excessive exclamation marks
- agency jargon

Shorter is usually better.

---

# 10. Success criteria

The final site should make it comfortable for Tre to:
- cold-email an established service-business owner and send them directly to the site
- quote $3,000+ for a website without the portfolio undermining the price
- show a contractor/service-business buyer immediately relevant proof
- show a local-brand buyer that the practice is not contractor-only
- send an OEM/engineering manager directly to `/controls` and look unquestionably technical
- present broad capability without appearing scattered
- remain visibly one independent professional rather than pretending to be an agency

Do not optimize for maximum apparent scale.
Optimize for a small, credible, premium independent practice that sells good fixed-scope work and protects clean handoffs.
